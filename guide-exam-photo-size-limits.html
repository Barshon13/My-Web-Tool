Exam Photo Size Limits Explained: 20 KB, 50 KB or 100 KB?

Guide • Updated: 2026-11-11 • Free, in your browser — your photos are never uploaded

Every exam form asks for a photo — and almost every form asks for a different file size. One notification says 20 KB, the next says 50 KB, another allows 100 KB. Upload the wrong size and the form rejects your photo, sometimes without telling you why.

This guide explains the three limits you will see most often, how to find out which one your form wants, and how to hit it exactly.

Quick facts — exam photos (NEET, JEE, GATE, SSC CGL)

  • Dimensions: 35×45 mm (413×531 px)
  • File size: 20 KB – 50 KB for most NTA and board exams
  • Resolution: 300 DPI, portrait 7:9
  • Background: white or light background, 80% face coverage, ears visible
  • Rule: always follow your own form’s notification — it wins over any guide

The three limits, and who uses them

20 KB — the strict limit. Many SSC and bank recruitment forms cap the photo at 20 KB. These are the hardest to hit, because a sharp colour photo is naturally bigger than 20 KB. You need proper compression, not just a smaller image.

50 KB — the common limit. NEET, JEE and most NTA exams accept photos between 20 KB and 50 KB. This is the most forgiving of the three: a well-taken 35×45 mm photo at 300 DPI usually fits after one round of compression.

100 KB — the generous limit. Some university and state-level forms allow up to 100 KB. If your form says 100 KB, do not over-compress — a 60–90 KB photo will look much sharper than a 20 KB one, and it still passes.

Step 1: Read your own notification first

Before you resize anything, open the official notification, information bulletin or admit-card instructions for your exam and find the photo section. It will state the dimensions, the KB range and the background colour. Write those numbers down — they are the only ones that matter for your form.

Step 2: Start from a good original

Take a fresh photo against a white or light background, in bright even light, with your face covering about 80% of the frame and both ears visible. A good original compresses cleanly; a dark or blurry original turns to mush the moment you shrink it.

Step 4: Verify before you upload

Check the downloaded file: its size should be inside your form’s range, the face should still be sharp when you zoom in, and the background should be white or light. If the file is under the limit but looks blurry, start again from the original photo instead of compressing the same file twice.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Guessing the limit — a photo made for 50 KB will be rejected by a 20 KB form, and vice versa.
  • Using a selfie with a busy background — exam forms want a plain background and a front-facing photo.
  • Screenshotting your photo to make it smaller — screenshots lose quality and often make the file bigger, not smaller.
  • Compressing the same file again and again — every round of compression adds blur. Always start from the original.
  • Forgetting the name and date stamp — some forms ask for a name and date printed on the photo, so check the notification.
