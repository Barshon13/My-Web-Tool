Images to PDF for Job Applications (A4, No Watermark)

Guide • Updated: 2026-11-11 • Free, in your browser — your photos are never uploaded

Job applications usually want your documents as one PDF file — your photo, your certificates, your ID — all in a single document you can attach to a form or an email. Sending ten separate photos looks messy, and some forms only accept one file.

This guide shows you how to turn your images into one clean A4 PDF, with no watermark and no software to install.

Quick facts — images to PDF

  • Page size: A4, the standard for job applications
  • Watermark: none — the PDF is clean
  • Cost: free, in your browser, no upload
  • Best for: job applications, college admissions, document sets

Step 1: Gather your images in order

Decide the order first: photo first, then certificates in date order, then your ID last is a common layout for job applications. Rename or sort the files so they are in that order on your device — the PDF will keep the order you add them in, so getting this right now saves you from rebuilding the file later.

Step 2: Open the PDF Creator and add your images

Open ResizeHub’s Images to PDF tool and add your images. You can add photos, scanned certificates and ID cards — JPG and PNG both work. Check that each image is the right way up; a sideways certificate is easy to miss until the PDF is already made.

Step 3: Choose A4 and fit each image to the page

Set the page size to A4. If your images are different sizes, use the “Fit to Image Size” option so each page matches its image instead of stretching it, or keep a fixed A4 layout with margins for a uniform look. Either way, the text on your certificates should stay readable — if it is too small to read, that page needs a closer crop before you add it.

Step 4: Compile and download the PDF

Press the compile button and download your PDF. Open it and flip through every page: the order should be right, every page should be the right way up, and the text should be sharp. Check the file size too — if you are attaching it to a form, most forms accept a few MB, but if it is very large, compress the images first and rebuild.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Wrong order — a certificate before the photo, or an ID in the middle, looks careless. Sort first.
  • Sideways pages — rotate the image before adding it, not after the PDF is made.
  • Tiny, unreadable text — if a certificate is unreadable at A4 size, scan it again at higher quality.
  • Mixing huge uncompressed scans — a few big scans make a giant PDF that forms reject. Compress the images first.
  • Paying for a converter that adds a watermark — a watermark on a job application looks unprofessional, and you do not need to pay for this at all.

One clean A4 PDF, in the right order, with readable pages and no watermark — that is all a job application needs. Your images are processed entirely in your browser and never uploaded anywhere.
