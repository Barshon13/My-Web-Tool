US Visa Photo (DS-160) & DV Lottery Photo Requirements

Guide • Updated: 2026-11-24 • Free, in your browser — your photos are never uploaded

The US visa photo looks simple — a square photo on a white background — but the rules behind it are exact, and getting them wrong has real consequences. On the DS-160, a photo that fails the size or format check blocks the form until you fix it. In the Diversity Visa Lottery, a photo that breaks the rules can disqualify your entry outright, and you may never be told why. The good news is that one digital specification from the US Department of State covers the DS-160, the DS-260 and DV entries, so once you understand it, you can make a photo that works for all of them.

This guide explains that specification, the composition rules, and the extra DV Lottery rules that catch people out — including the photo size to use, who needs a photo, and the new registration fee and passport scan. Rules change between cycles — always confirm against the official notification or portal before you submit.

Quick facts — the US visa photo

  • Digital file: square JPEG, 600×600 px minimum to 1200×1200 px maximum, 240 KB or less, 24-bit colour in sRGB, JPEG compression ratio 20:1 or less
  • Printed photo: 2×2 inches (51×51 mm); scanning a print at 300 pixels per inch gives exactly 600×600 px
  • Head size: 50–69% of the image height, chin to top of hair; eyes 56–69% of the height from the bottom
  • Background: plain white or off-white; photo taken within the last 6 months
  • Glasses: not allowed since 1 November 2016, except for a documented medical reason
  • DV Lottery: make the file exactly 600×600 px; one photo per person; never reuse last year’s photo

The digital specification — one rule for DS-160, DS-260 and DV

The US Department of State (travel.state.gov) sets one digital specification that covers the DS-160, the DS-260 and DV entries. The file must be a square JPEG, from 600×600 px minimum to 1200×1200 px maximum, 240 KB or less, in 24-bit colour in sRGB, with a JPEG compression ratio of 20:1 or less. In practice, that means: save as JPEG, keep it square, keep it at or under 240 KB, and do not over-compress it. A file that is too large, the wrong shape, or saved in the wrong format will fail the upload check. The printed photo is 2×2 inches (51×51 mm). If you are starting from a printed photo, scanning it at 300 pixels per inch gives exactly 600×600 px, which is the minimum digital size and the size the DV form names.

Composition — head size, background and expression

The composition rules are just as strict as the file rules. The photo must have been taken within the last 6 months, so it shows how you look now. The background must be plain white or off-white, with no patterns, shadows or other people in the frame. Face the camera directly with a full face, a neutral expression and both eyes open. Your head must take up 50–69% of the image height, measured from chin to the top of your hair, and your eyes must sit 56–69% of the height from the bottom of the image. That head-size range is the part most home photos get wrong: too far from the camera and your head is too small; too close and it fills the frame. Take the photo with the camera at eye level, in bright, even light, and check the head size before you crop — cropping cannot fix a head that was far too small in the original shot.

Glasses — not allowed since 1 November 2016

Do not wear glasses in a US visa photo. Glasses have not been allowed since 1 November 2016, except where there is a documented medical reason. This surprises people who wear glasses every day, but the rule is clear: take the photo without them. Sunglasses, tinted lenses and glare across the eyes are also out. If you normally wear glasses, take them off for the photo and put them back on afterwards — the photo only needs to show your face clearly, not how you usually look.

DV Lottery specifics — exactly 600×600 px, one photo per person

The DV Lottery has its own details on top of the general specification. The DV entry instructions name exactly 600×600 px. The general digital spec allows up to 1200×1200 px, but the DV form names 600×600 — so make the file exactly 600×600 px to be safe, rather than uploading a larger file and hoping the form accepts it. You need one photo per person: yourself, your spouse, and every unmarried child under 21, even if they do not plan to immigrate. Each of those photos must meet the same rules — recent, white background, correct head size, no glasses. Never reuse last year’s photo: a reused photo is one of the classic DV mistakes, and the photo must be from within the last 6 months in any case.

Two newer DV rules are also worth knowing. Since the DV-2027 entry period, the State Department charges a $1 registration fee and asks for a scan of the entrant’s valid passport biographic page. Submit only on the official site, dvprogram.state.gov — other sites that claim to enter you in the lottery are not the official entry route. If you are selected, printed 2×2 inch photos are needed later for the DS-260 interview, so keep a printed copy as well as the digital file.

DS-160 — the form checks your photo automatically

On the DS-160, the photo check is automatic: the form checks the photo as you upload it, and a file that fails the size or format check blocks the form until you fix it. That is frustrating in the middle of an application, so fix the photo before you start the form. Make the file to the specification above, check its size and dimensions on your device, and only then begin the DS-160. Keep a printed 2×2 inch copy for the interview as well — the digital upload does not replace the print you may be asked to bring. The same applies to the DS-260: the digital specification is the same, so one correctly made file can serve both forms, as long as it is still recent when you use it.

How to make the photo

Take a fresh photo against a plain white or off-white wall in bright, even daylight, with the camera at eye level and no glasses on. Keep a neutral expression and look straight at the camera. For a DV entry, use exactly 600×600 px. If the file is over 240 KB, compress it to an exact KB target rather than guessing a quality slider, and check the result at full size to make sure your face is still sharp.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Uploading a 1200×1200 px file to the DV Lottery — the DV instructions name exactly 600×600 px, so make it exactly that size.
  • Reusing last year’s photo — DV entries and visa photos must be from within the last 6 months.
  • Forgetting a photo for a child or spouse — the DV entry needs one photo per person, including every unmarried child under 21.
  • Wearing glasses — not allowed since 1 November 2016, except for a documented medical reason.
  • A head that is too small or too large — it must be 50–69% of the image height, with eyes 56–69% from the bottom.
  • Entering on a non-official site — submit DV entries only on dvprogram.state.gov.
  • Starting the DS-160 before fixing the photo — a failed photo check blocks the form until it is fixed.
