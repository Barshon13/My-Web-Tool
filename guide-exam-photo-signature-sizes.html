Exam Photo & Signature Sizes: SSC, UPSC, IBPS, Railways, NEET, JEE, GATE

Guide • Updated: 2026-11-24 • Free, in your browser — your photos are never uploaded

Every Indian exam asks for a photo and a signature, and almost none of them ask for the same sizes. A photo that passes for IBPS will be rejected by GATE, a signature that fits NEET will be too small for the Railways, and a file that worked last year may fail this year because the limits changed. That is why so many forms get stuck at the upload step — not because the candidate did anything wrong, but because they reused a file from a different exam without checking.

This guide lists the photo and signature sizes for SSC, UPSC, IBPS, Railways, NEET, JEE and GATE in one place, using only the figures from the official notifications and bulletins. Read the section for your exam, make a fresh file to those exact limits, and check the file size before you upload it. And remember: rules change between cycles — always confirm against the official notification or portal before you submit.

Quick facts — the sizes at a glance

  • UPSC CSE 2026: photo JPG 20–200 KB (no pixel size in the current instruction); signature JPG 20–100 KB, 350–500 px
  • IBPS: photo JPG 200×230 px, 20–50 KB; signature JPG 140×60 px, 10–20 KB
  • Railways RRB NTPC 2026: photo captured live in the form — no file upload; signature JPG 30–49 KB, at least 140×60 px
  • NEET (UG) 2026: photo JPG 10–200 KB; signature JPG 10–100 KB (older guides cite 4–30 KB)
  • JEE Main 2026: photo JPG 10–200 KB; signature JPG 10–100 KB (older guides cite 4–30 KB)
  • GATE 2026: photo JPG 5–600 KB, 200×260 to 530×690 px; signature JPG 3–300 KB, 250×80 to 580×180 px
  • SSC: signature under 20 KB; photo captured live in the form since 2026 — check your notification

SSC — signature under 20 KB, photo captured live

For SSC, the signature must be under 20 KB. The full step-by-step for scanning, cropping and compressing it is in our SSC signature guide, linked below. The photo side has changed recently: SSC has moved to capturing the photo live in the form since 2026, so you may not need to upload a photo file at all. Flag this as something to check in your own notification, because it is a recent change and the exact process can differ between SSC exams. Do not assume last year's upload step still applies.

UPSC CSE 2026 — photo 20–200 KB, signature signed three times

For UPSC CSE 2026, the photo must be a JPG of 20–200 KB. The current upload instruction states no pixel size for the photo. That matters, because an older reading exists: the retired OTR FAQ quoted 350–1000 px and 20–300 KB. If you see those older figures on a coaching site or in an old PDF, treat them as the older reading and follow the current instruction in your own notification instead. The signature is a JPG of 20–100 KB at 350–500 px. It must be signed three times, one below the other, in black ink on plain white paper, and saved as one image. A live photograph is also captured in the form, so the uploaded photo is not the only photo UPSC holds. Source: upsconline.nic.in and the UPSC CSE 2026 notice.

IBPS PO, Clerk and RRB — the most detailed checklist

IBPS (and SBI) ask for four separate uploads, and each one has its own size. The photo is JPG 200×230 px and 20–50 KB. The signature is JPG 140×60 px and 10–20 KB, signed in black ink on white paper, and not in capital letters — a signature in all capitals is a common reason for rejection. The left thumb impression is JPG 240×240 px and 20–50 KB. The handwritten declaration is JPG 800×400 px and 50–100 KB, written in English in your own handwriting. Because there are four files, the safest approach is to prepare and check each one separately, and to name the files clearly (photo, signature, thumb, declaration) so you do not upload the wrong one into the wrong slot. Source: IBPS CRP notifications.

Railways RRB NTPC 2026 — live photo, strict signature range

For RRB NTPC 2026 (CEN 06/2026 Graduate and CEN 07/2026 Undergraduate), the photograph is captured live by webcam or phone camera inside the form — no photo file is uploaded at all. Sit in bright, even light, face the camera directly, and keep the frame steady when the form takes the picture. The signature is different: it is uploaded as a JPG of 30–49 KB, at least 140×60 px, scanned at 100 DPI or more, in black ink on white paper, in running handwriting, signed inside a 35×20 mm box. Note the lower limit as well as the upper one — files under 30 KB are also rejected, so a signature that is too small fails just as surely as one that is too big. If your scan comes out under 30 KB, rescan it at a higher resolution or with less compression until it lands inside the 30–49 KB range.

NEET (UG) 2026 — photo 10–200 KB, taken after 1 January 2026

For NEET (UG) 2026, the NTA bulletin asks for a photo in JPG of 10–200 KB: passport size, with 80% face, ears visible, on a white background, and taken after 1 January 2026. A postcard photo of 4×6 inches is also required, also 10–200 KB. The signature is JPG 10–100 KB. An older reading exists here too: older guides cite 4–30 KB for the signature, so if you see that figure, treat it as the older reading and follow the current bulletin. Finger and thumb impressions are JPG 10–200 KB. The bulletin gives no pixel size for any of these, so do not invent one — meet the KB limit and the composition rules (face size, ears visible, white background) instead. Source: NTA NEET (UG) 2026 bulletin.

JEE Main 2026 — photo 10–200 KB, passport size 3.5×4.5 cm

For JEE Main 2026, the NTA bulletin asks for a photo in JPG of 10–200 KB, passport size 3.5×4.5 cm, with 80% face on a white background. The signature is JPG 10–100 KB, and again an older reading exists: older guides cite 4–30 KB, so treat that as the older reading and follow the current bulletin. As with NEET, the bulletin gives no pixel size, so the KB limit and the composition rules are what you need to meet. If you are applying for both NEET and JEE, do not assume one set of files works for both — prepare each form's files against its own bulletin, even when the limits look the same. Source: NTA JEE Main 2026 bulletin.

GATE 2026 — the widest ranges, with ratios to match

GATE 2026, run by IIT Guwahati, gives the widest ranges of any exam here, and it also gives ratios. The photo is JPG 5–600 KB, 200×260 to 530×690 px, with a width-to-height ratio of 0.66–0.89, sized 3.5×4.5 cm, with the face covering 60–70% of the image, on a white background. The signature is JPG 3–300 KB, 250×80 to 580×180 px, with a width-to-height ratio of 2.75–3.75, in black or dark blue ink, with the signature covering 70–80% of the image. The ratio matters: a signature that is the right number of pixels but too tall or too wide for its ratio can still fail. When you crop, keep the signature filling most of the frame — a small signature floating in a large white box will not meet the 70–80% coverage rule. Source: gate2026.iitg.ac.in.

How to get the sizes right

Start from a sharp original. A blurry photo compressed to the right KB size is still a blurry photo, and a signature scanned at low resolution will not get sharper by resizing it. Scan signatures at 100 DPI or more on plain white paper, in black ink (or dark blue where the exam allows it), and crop tightly so the signature fills the frame. Then set the exact KB target in the resizer instead of guessing a quality slider, and verify the file size on your device before you upload it — check the file properties, not just what the tool page says. Finally, read your own notification first. Limits change every cycle, and this guide can only repeat what the current notifications say. Rules change between cycles — always confirm against the official notification or portal before you submit.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Reusing a file from a different exam — IBPS, GATE and NEET limits are all different, so a file that passed one form can fail another.
  • Signing in capital letters — IBPS rejects signatures in capitals; sign in your normal running handwriting.
  • Ignoring the lower KB limit — RRB rejects signatures under 30 KB, so too small fails as well as too big.
  • Using an old photo — NEET wants a photo taken after 1 January 2026, and most exams want a recent photo.
  • Leaving the signature small in a big white box — GATE wants the signature to cover 70–80% of the image.
  • Uploading the wrong file into the wrong slot — with four IBPS uploads, label each file clearly before you start.

Frequently asked questions

What size should my SSC signature be?

Your SSC signature must be under 20 KB. See our SSC signature guide for the exact steps, and check your own notification, because SSC has moved to capturing the photo live in the form since 2026.

Does UPSC ask for a pixel size for the CSE photo?

The current UPSC CSE 2026 upload instruction states a JPG photo of 20-200 KB and no pixel size. The retired OTR FAQ quoted 350-1000 px and 20-300 KB, so if you see that older figure, treat it as the older reading and follow your own notification.

What are the IBPS photo and signature sizes?

For IBPS PO, Clerk and RRB, the photo is JPG 200x230 px and 20-50 KB, and the signature is JPG 140x60 px and 10-20 KB, signed in black ink on white paper and not in capital letters.

Is the RRB NTPC photo uploaded as a file?

No. For RRB NTPC 2026, the photograph is captured live by webcam or phone camera inside the form, so no photo file is uploaded. Only the signature is uploaded as a file.
