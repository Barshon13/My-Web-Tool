Schengen vs UK Visa Photo Size: 35×45 mm or 600×750 px?

Guide • Updated: 2026-11-11 • Free, in your browser — your photos are never uploaded

Visa photos are not one-size-fits-all. A Schengen visa wants a 35×45 mm photo, a UK passport wants 600×750 px minimum, and a US visa wants a square 2×2 inch photo. Use the wrong size and the visa centre will ask you to retake the photo — which costs you a day.

This guide compares the three most common visa photo sizes so you can make the right one the first time.

Quick facts — compare the sizes

  • Schengen visa: 35×45 mm (413×531 px), under 200 KB, 300 DPI, light grey or pure white background
  • UK passport: 600×750 px minimum, 50 KB – 10 MB, 300 DPI, plain light-coloured background
  • US visa: 2×2 in (600×600 px), under 240 KB, 300 DPI, plain white or off-white background

Schengen visa photo — 35×45 mm

The Schengen photo is a portrait rectangle, 35 mm wide and 45 mm tall (413×531 px). The file must be under 200 KB at 300 DPI. Your face must be 32–36 mm tall in the photo — about 70–80% of the height — and the photo must have been taken within the last 6 months. The background should be light grey or pure white, and your face should be front-facing with a neutral expression.

UK passport photo — 600×750 px

The UK digital passport photo is also portrait, but bigger: at least 600×750 px at 300 DPI, with a file size between 50 KB and 10 MB. The background must be plain and light-coloured. The UK rules are strict about quality: no red-eye, sharp focus, and no digital beauty filters or retouching of any kind.

US visa photo — 2×2 inch square

The US photo is different again — a square, 2×2 inches (600×600 px, up to 1200×1200 px), under 240 KB, on a plain white or off-white background. Your head should take up 50–69% of the height, with no glasses and a neutral expression.

How to make the right one

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using one photo for every country — Schengen, UK and US sizes are all different.
  • Using an old photo — Schengen photos must be less than 6 months old, and most countries want a recent photo.
  • Retouching or filtering the photo — the UK and Schengen rules both reject edited photos.
  • A dark or shadowy background — every visa photo needs a plain, light background.
  • Smiling or looking away — keep a neutral expression and look straight at the camera.
