How to Make a 600×600 px Passport Photo at Home (2×2 Inch)

Guide • Updated: 2026-11-11 • Free, in your browser — your photos are never uploaded

A standard passport photo is a 2×2 inch square — 600×600 pixels at 300 DPI. You do not need a studio to get one. With a phone camera, a white wall and a few minutes, you can make a photo that meets the official rules, right at home.

This guide walks you through the exact requirements and the steps, so your photo is accepted the first time.

Quick facts — US / international passport photo

  • Size: 2×2 in (600×600 px), up to 1200×1200 px
  • File size: under 240 KB
  • Resolution: 300 DPI, square 1:1
  • Background: plain white or off-white
  • Face: no glasses, neutral expression, head between 50–69% of the photo height

Step 1: Find a plain white wall

Stand about one to two feet in front of a flat, plain white or off-white wall. The wall is your background, so it must be empty — no pictures, no switches, no shadows. Daylight from a window in front of you gives the most even light.

Step 2: Set up the camera at eye level

Ask someone to hold the phone, or prop it up on a shelf about four to five feet away, exactly at your eye level. Do not hold the phone yourself at arm’s length — the angle makes your face look distorted. Look straight into the lens with a neutral expression and both eyes open.

Step 3: Take the photo, then crop it square

Take a few shots and pick the sharpest one. Then open ResizeHub’s Passport Photo Maker and upload it. Crop it to a square, with your head centred and taking up roughly half to two-thirds of the frame. Your ears should be visible and your shoulders should just appear at the bottom edge.

Step 4: Fix the background and the size

Use the white-background option so the wall behind you becomes clean white. Then set the output to 600×600 px at 300 DPI. The tool compresses the file to under 240 KB automatically — if your photo is larger, it will shrink the file without making your face blurry.

Step 5: Check before you submit

Before you upload the photo to your application, zoom in and check four things: the background is plain white, your face is sharp and front-facing, there are no shadows on the wall, and the file is under 240 KB. If anything looks off, retake the photo — it is faster than getting the application sent back.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Wearing glasses or a hat — most passport rules do not allow them.
  • Smiling or tilting your head — keep a neutral expression and look straight ahead.
  • A coloured or busy background — it is the most common reason for rejection.
  • A dark or shadowy photo — stand facing the light, not with the light behind you.
  • Using an old photo with a filter — no beauty filters, no retouching, and the photo should be recent.

Once your 600×600 px photo is ready, you can use the same steps for most visa and ID forms too — only the exact size changes. Your photo is processed entirely in your browser and never uploaded anywhere.
