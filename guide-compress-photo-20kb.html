How to Compress a Photo to Exactly 20 KB Without Blur

Guide • Updated: 2026-11-11 • Free, in your browser — your photos are never uploaded

SSC forms, bank forms and exam portals all love the number 20 KB. Your photo has to fit under it — but if you just shrink the quality blindly, the photo turns blurry and the form still might reject it for poor quality.

This guide shows you how to get a photo under 20 KB and keep it sharp, and how the compressor on this site does the hard part for you.

Quick facts — compressing to 20 KB

  • Target: under 20 KB (aim for 15–19 KB, not exactly 20)
  • Best format: JPG for photos — it compresses far better than PNG
  • Start right: a sharp, well-lit original compresses cleanly; a blurry one does not
  • Never: compress the same file twice — always start from the original

Why photos get blurry when you compress them

A photo file is mostly detail. When you lower the JPEG quality, the encoder throws away fine detail — first in the smooth areas, then in faces and edges. If you drop the quality too far in one step, the face goes soft and blocky. The trick is to lower the quality gradually and stop as soon as the file fits under 20 KB, keeping as much quality as possible.

Step 1: Start from the best original you have

Use the original photo from your camera, not a copy that was already sent on WhatsApp or compressed before. Messaging apps shrink photos before they send them, so a photo that has already been through WhatsApp has already lost the detail you need. If you only have the WhatsApp copy, take a fresh photo if you can.

Step 2: Crop first, then compress

Crop the photo to the size your form wants before you compress it. A cropped photo has fewer pixels, so it needs less compression to fit under 20 KB — and the face stays sharper. For exam and passport photos, crop to the right ratio first (for example 35×45 mm), then set the KB target.

Step 3: Set the target to 20 KB and let the tool work

Open ResizeHub’s Image Compressor, upload your photo and set the target to 20 KB. The tool tries different quality levels one by one — a binary search — until it finds the highest quality that still fits under 20 KB. That is much more accurate than guessing a quality percentage yourself.

Step 4: Check the result with your own eyes

Download the compressed photo and zoom in on the face. It should still be sharp, with no blocky squares around the eyes or edges. If it looks soft, the original was probably too small or too dark — go back to the original photo, brighten it a little, and try again. Do not compress the already-compressed file a second time; that only adds blur.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Compressing a PNG photo — PNG files are huge for photos. Convert to JPG first and the same photo will be far smaller.
  • Screenshotting to shrink a photo — screenshots add blur and often make the file bigger.
  • Aiming for exactly 20.0 KB — aim for 15–19 KB instead, so a small rounding difference never pushes you over the limit.
  • Starting from a dark, grainy photo — grain is detail the encoder has to store, so dark photos compress badly. Take the photo in good light.

That is the whole method: good original, crop first, set the 20 KB target, check the face. Your photo stays sharp, the form accepts it, and the whole thing is done in your browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.
