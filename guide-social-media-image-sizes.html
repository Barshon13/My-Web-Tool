Social Media Image Sizes Cheat Sheet 2026

Guide • Updated: 2026-11-24 • Free, in your browser — your photos are never uploaded

Every platform crops differently, and one image rarely fits them all. Post a square photo to Instagram and it looks fine; post the same square to a Facebook link preview and it gets cropped; use it as a YouTube thumbnail and it is the wrong shape entirely. The fix is not to guess — it is to start from the size each platform actually wants, and to resize once per platform before you post.

This cheat sheet lists the sizes for Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, YouTube, TikTok and Pinterest in one place. The sizes were checked against platform help pages in October 2026 — and platforms change sizes, so if a size is rejected, check in-app. Rules change between cycles — always confirm against the official notification or portal before you submit.

Quick facts — the sizes most people need

  • Instagram post: square 1080×1080 (1:1); portrait 1080×1350 (4:5); Story and Reel 1080×1920 (9:16)
  • Facebook post: 1080×1350 or 1080×1080; shared link image 1200×630; Page cover 851×315
  • LinkedIn post: 1200×627; profile photo at least 400×400 (up to 8 MB)
  • X post image: 1600×900 or 1200×675; header 1500×500
  • YouTube thumbnail: 1280×720 (16:9), under 2 MB; channel banner 2560×1440
  • TikTok: vertical 1080×1920 (9:16); profile picture 200×200
  • Pinterest Pin: standard 1000×1500 (2:3); square 1000×1000

Instagram — square, portrait, landscape, Story and Reel

Instagram gives you the most shapes to choose from, which is why it causes the most confusion. A square post is 1080×1080 px (1:1). A portrait post is 1080×1350 px (4:5) — taller than square, and the shape that takes up the most space in the feed. A tall portrait is 1080×1440 px (3:4). A landscape post is 1080×566 px (1.91:1), wide and short. Stories and Reels are both 1080×1920 px (9:16), full-screen vertical. A Reel cover is 420×654 px, and a profile picture is 320×320 px. If you only remember one Instagram rule, remember this: portrait 1080×1350 gets you the most feed space, and vertical 1080×1920 is the only shape for Stories and Reels. Everything else is a variation on those two.

Facebook — posts, link images, covers and Stories

On Facebook, an image post is 1080×1350 px or 1080×1080 px — the same portrait and square shapes as Instagram, which makes it easy to reuse one image for both. A shared link image is 1200×630 px, at least 600×315 px, and up to 8 MB — this is the preview image that appears when you share a link, and it is wider than a post image, so do not reuse a square post image for it or the preview will crop awkwardly. A Page cover is 851×315 px, wide and short, and a Story is 1080×1920 px (9:16), the same vertical shape as Instagram Stories. Covers are the ones people get wrong most often: they design a cover at post size, upload it, and find the top and bottom cut off. Design covers at their own size, and keep important text away from the edges.

LinkedIn — posts, profile, covers and articles

LinkedIn is the most formal of the platforms here, and its sizes reflect that. An image post is 1200×627 px, a wide landscape shape. A profile photo must be at least 400×400 px and up to 8 MB — square, and large enough to stay sharp when it appears small next to your name. A personal cover is 1584×396 px, and a company Page cover is 1512×256 px — note that the two covers are different sizes, so a personal cover will not fit a company Page cleanly. An article cover is 1920×1080 px. Because LinkedIn posts are landscape, a portrait photo from Instagram will be cropped hard if you reuse it directly — resize it to 1200×627 first, and check that faces and text survive the crop.

X — profile, header and post images

X keeps its list short. A profile photo is 400×400 px, square. A header is 1500×500 px, wide and short — and because the profile photo overlaps the bottom-left of the header, keep important text out of that corner. A post image is 1600×900 px or 1200×675 px, both 16:9 landscape. That 16:9 shape is the key difference from Instagram: X post images are wide, not square or portrait, so a square image posted to X will have empty space on the sides or get cropped. If you are posting the same image to Instagram and X, make two versions — a square or portrait one for Instagram, and a 16:9 one for X.

YouTube — thumbnails, banners and profile pictures

YouTube has three sizes that matter, and the thumbnail is the most important of them. A thumbnail is 1280×720 px (16:9), at least 640 px wide, and under 2 MB. It is small on screen but it decides whether someone clicks, so make it at exactly 1280×720 and keep the text large enough to read at thumbnail size. A channel banner is 2560×1440 px, at least 2048×1152 px, 6 MB or less — and you must keep text inside the 1546×423 px safe area in the middle, because the banner is cropped differently on phones, tablets, TVs and desktops, and anything outside that safe area may not be seen. A profile picture is 800×800 px, square. The banner safe area is the detail most people miss: a banner designed edge-to-edge looks fine on a desktop and loses its text on a phone.

TikTok and Pinterest — vertical video and tall Pins

TikTok is vertical-first: a vertical photo or video is 1080×1920 px (9:16), the same full-screen shape as Instagram Stories and Reels, and a profile picture is 200×200 px. Anything landscape on TikTok will look small and out of place, so shoot and resize vertical. Pinterest is the opposite habit: a standard Pin is 1000×1500 px (2:3), tall but not full-screen vertical, and a square Pin is 1000×1000 px. The 2:3 ratio is the one to use for standard Pins — taller Pins get cropped in the feed, and square Pins take up less space. If you make infographics or product images for Pinterest, design at 1000×1500 from the start rather than resizing a square image and hoping the text survives.

Practical tips — work at 1080 px, mind the safe zones, pick the right format

A few habits make all of these sizes easier. Work at 1080 px wide for most posts: it is the standard width for Instagram and Facebook posts and Stories, and it is sharp enough for every feed on this list. Keep text inside safe zones: Stories crop on some screens, YouTube banners crop on phones, and X headers are partly covered by the profile photo — so keep headlines, logos and calls to action away from the edges and out of the corners. Export JPG for photos and PNG for graphics with text: JPG keeps photographs small, while PNG keeps sharp text and lines clean where JPG would smear them. And when one image has to go to several platforms, resize a copy for each platform rather than uploading one size everywhere — a few minutes of resizing is cheaper than a cropped face or a cut-off headline on the platform that matters most.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using one size everywhere — a square Instagram post is the wrong shape for a YouTube thumbnail, an X post image or a LinkedIn post.
  • Designing a cover at post size — Facebook Page covers, LinkedIn covers and YouTube banners each have their own size and safe area.
  • Putting text at the edges — Stories crop on some screens, banners crop on phones, and the X profile photo covers part of the header.
  • Exporting graphics as JPG — text and sharp lines go soft in JPG; use PNG for graphics with text.
  • Using an old size after a platform changes it — sizes were checked in October 2026, so if a size is rejected, check in-app for the current size.
