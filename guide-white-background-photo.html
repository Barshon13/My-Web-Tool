How to Get a White Background for Passport and Exam Photos

Guide • Updated: 2026-11-11 • Free, in your browser — your photos are never uploaded

The single most common reason a passport or exam photo gets rejected is the background. The rules all say the same thing — plain white — and a photo taken in your bedroom with a coloured wall behind you will not pass.

The good news: getting a white background is mostly about how you take the photo, not about editing. This guide shows you both — how to shoot it right, and how to fix it in one click if the wall was not perfect.

Quick facts — background rules by form

  • Passport / US visa: plain white or off-white
  • Exam photos (NEET, JEE): white or light background
  • Schengen visa: light grey or pure white
  • UK passport: plain light-coloured background
  • Signatures: pure white paper

Step 1: Find a white wall, not a white sheet

A flat, plain white wall is the easiest background there is. A bedsheet sounds like a good idea, but sheets wrinkle, and every wrinkle casts a shadow that shows up in the photo. A wall is flat, even and shadow-free — use the wall if you possibly can.

Step 2: Stand one to two feet from the wall

Stand about one to two feet in front of the wall, facing a bright window or a lamp in front of you. The gap matters: if you lean against the wall, your head casts a shadow on it, and that shadow is the first thing a reviewer notices. Light from the front keeps both sides of your face evenly lit and keeps the wall bright and clean.

Step 3: Take the photo straight on

Hold the phone at eye level, about four to five feet away, and take the photo straight on — not from above, not from below. Look straight into the lens with a neutral expression. Take a few shots and pick the sharpest one with the most even light.

Step 4: Make the background white in one click

Open ResizeHub’s Passport Photo Maker, upload your photo and use the white-background option. It turns the wall behind you into clean white while keeping your face and hair natural. Then crop to the size your form wants — for example 600×600 px for a passport photo, or 35×45 mm for an exam photo — and download it.

Step 5: Check the edges before you submit

Zoom in on the finished photo and look at the edges around your hair and shoulders. The background should be evenly white, with no grey patches, no shadow behind your head, and no leftover pieces of wall or furniture at the corners. If you can see a shadow, retake the photo standing a little further from the wall.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • A coloured or patterned wall — even a light blue wall can get a photo rejected.
  • Standing against the wall — your head shadow lands right behind you.
  • Light from behind you — your face goes dark and the wall goes grey.
  • Over-whitening the photo — if the background tool is pushed too far, the edges of your hair can disappear. Use the preset, then check the edges.
  • Using a photo with a busy background and hoping editing will save it — a clean wall at the start beats any amount of editing later.
